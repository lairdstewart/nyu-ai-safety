LASR projects in AI safety conferences

Five of LASR’s papers from past cohorts were recently accepted into NeurIPS 2026 and ICML 2026, leading conferences for AI safety research:
  • EvalDetectBench: A Benchmark for Measuring Evaluation Awareness in Frontier Language Models
  • The Model Organism Lottery: Model Organism Interpretability Strongly Depends on Training Methodology
  • ToyBench: A Less Toy Model of Superposition
  • Phantom Transfer: Data-level Defences are Insufficient Against Data Poisoning
  • Evaluating and Understanding Scheming Propensity in LLM Agents

LASR is the only technical AI safety fellowship to require a publication as an output. 50% of all papers produced at LASR have been accepted to top technical AI safety research conferences.

Calling experts

We are looking for people whose work or research has given them a well-grounded understanding of how advanced AI could go wrong, or of how governments respond to crises.

Alignment Team
  • Our Alignment team has recently published its first big research project: Stress Testing Alignment Midtraining. We found that alignment midtraining works under idealised conditions, but it cracks under pressure, such as when the post-training data is incomplete or conflicts with the target motivation.
    Our findings
  • Since frontier labs don’t openly publish evidence that their alignment techniques are robust across a wide range of training and deployment contexts, we believe it is important to publicly reproduce and stress-test these safety measures.
